# Prepare a Working Notes Substack post

Use this guide to turn saved Public Job Feed reports into a draft for Working
Notes. Read the current ChatGPT project instructions and its Library copy of
Adam Pugh's Substack Voice Charter before drafting. Use Adam's current request
first. Use the project instructions for post type, title, distribution, and
support copy. Use the Charter for voice and editing.

The Charter supplied for this review on October 15, 2026 is marked **2.1 draft**.
That label does not establish approval of the revision. If required guidance
is unavailable, state the gap. Do not claim to have read it. Old articles and
the skill's older job-roundup template do not replace current instructions.
The supplied project instructions also end mid-sentence in the tool-review
template. Use the available rules and mark that gap when preparing a tool
review; do not invent the missing instruction.

## Choose the post type

| Request | Format |
| --- | --- |
| Broad weekly roundup | **Writing and documentation jobs**, with the subtitle **Week of [Month Day, Year]**. Combine useful feed statistics with compact links and general information about many types of jobs. Preserve the supplied report's scope, order, groups, and links. |
| Five recent roles in detail | **Five recent documentation jobs**. Use the [five-job guide](weekly-top-five-substack.md). Verify recent employer publication dates and give each role a short explanation of the work and important limits. This is a separate series. |
| Editing examples and follow-up | **Everyone needs an editor**. Use exact examples, dated evidence, context, and a useful editorial observation. Keep a company's response separate from a verified correction. |
| Feed metrics lookback | State the question, period, population, findings, and limits. Use the project's metrics template. |
| Process article, tool review, or personal observation | Let the material set the structure. Keep the shared voice and evidence rules. Do not require a full template for each type. |

The broad weekly roundup is a public post and email. For other post types,
use Adam's distribution choice. The trial plan below proposes email for the
five-job series and a link in the roundup for other articles. Use Adam's supplied edition
date. If it is missing, mark `[NEEDS EDITION DATE]`; do not substitute a package
date, today's date, or the start of a calendar week.

## Proposed publishing schedule

This is a four-week trial plan, not an approved publishing promise or an
automation. The days are editorial choices, not claims about the best time
to reach readers. Adam's request can change the plan.

| Series | Proposed frequency | Preparation and distribution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Writing and documentation jobs | Saturday, each week | Prepare from the latest suitable saved reports. Publish and email the broad list and useful statistics. |
| Five recent documentation jobs | Wednesday, each week during the trial | Verify recent dates and live applications close to publication. Publish and email five roles in detail. Skip the edition if five suitable roles cannot be verified. |
| Everyone needs an editor | About once a month, when evidence is ready | Publish the article and link to it in the next roundup. A separate email is an editorial choice. |
| Other articles and metrics lookbacks | When there is a useful result or observation | Publish when ready. Usually link from a job email rather than add another scheduled email. |

Review the plan after four weeks. Check preparation effort, evidence quality,
and whether both job series serve a distinct purpose. Reader metrics can
inform the review, but a change does not prove that the schedule caused it.

Keep a small publication log: series, edition date, source window, draft status,
published URL, email status, and any correction or follow-up. For five-job
posts, record application URLs and employer publication dates. Avoid featuring
the same role again without a useful update. A role can still appear in the
broad roundup because that list has a different purpose.

If data or checks are incomplete, delay the post or state the limit. Do not
force an edition to meet the trial schedule. Do not create recurring tasks or
publish automatically from this plan.

## Writing and documentation jobs

Use this order:

1. Title: **Writing and documentation jobs**.
2. Substack subtitle: **Week of [Adam's edition date]**.
3. Brief introduction, only when useful news or context is supplied.
4. A clear link to the free [Good Doc Jobs sheet](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1rECWXCGhDKUiB3-LIwEEe1teaPWaGxgSK28AZADFF4g/edit?usp=sharing).
5. A short metrics summary for this edition's population and dates.
6. The supplied listings, with their scope, order, and groups preserved.
7. A source note with check dates and important limits.
8. The latest approved support footer, separate from the article's conclusion.

Keep entries compact and consistent. Preserve exact role titles, actual
employer names, locations, work arrangements, and supplied application links.
Keep all links when one entry represents several source records. Flag duplicate
entries, unclear groups, and inconsistent totals before changing them. Do not
reduce the list to five jobs or add a review paragraph to every entry.

Treat generated Markdown as writing input. Its file heading and `Snapshot`
label do not set the edition's title or subtitle. Move source metadata into the
source note as needed, while retaining dates and limits. Do not claim that the
report generator already applies the current editorial template.

For weekly listings, use supplied checks and their actual dates. A writing
request alone does not require a feed refresh or new checks of every page.
For **Five recent documentation jobs**, verify the official detail and
application pages as described in the five-job guide. Keep its title and
short explanatory subtitle separate from the broad roundup's week label.

## Everyone needs an editor and other articles

Use the series title **Everyone needs an editor** consistently. A short subtitle
can name the company or the issue. Keep a light evidence checklist rather than
a fixed article structure:

- Save the exact passage, source URL, page context, and observation date.
- Confirm that the passage is an error in context. A deliberate teaching
  example, unfamiliar name, or duplicate page surface is not another mistake.
- Explain why the example matters. Do not infer a company's full editing
  process or staff ability from one page.
- Separate the original finding, any company response, and a later page check.
  Keep a short correction or follow-up note when the evidence changes.

Adam reports that Grammarly replied on LinkedIn and said the findings were
passed to the relevant teams. Obtain the exact comment and date before quoting
it. A referral to a team does not prove that the page was corrected or that the
team accepted every finding. Verify any claimed correction separately.

Process articles, reviews, and personal observations need the same care with
voice, facts, links, and support copy. Their openings, headings, and endings
can follow the material. Use examples to establish consistency without forcing
each article into a complete template.

## Metrics and source notes

- State the population and comparison dates. Give denominators for percentages.
- Reconcile work arrangements with the total, including Unknown where needed.
- Separate source-record counts, unique public jobs, and displayed bullets.
  Grouping can reduce the bullet count without changing the source count.
- Account for regional overlap before adding U.S. and international totals.
  Location markers do not establish hiring eligibility.
- Call daily changes **Added since previous package**. Use weekly wording only
  for a stated weekly comparison. Give the actual range and baseline.
- Distinguish jobs first found in saved history from employer posting dates.
  A removal is a membership change, not proof of closure or hiring.
- Compare editions only when their scope and definitions match. Explain changes
  in source coverage. Do not force different populations to have equal totals.
- Do not infer a market trend or a cause from changes in job or reader counts.

## Voice, access, and support

Use plain language and standard punctuation. Do not add em dashes to new prose,
or replace them with spaced hyphens or double hyphens. Preserve exact source
titles, quotations, URLs, and code. Keep supplied judgments and uncertainty.
Do not invent Adam's experience, reactions, jokes, or opinions.

Good Doc Jobs is free to use and share. No signup or subscription is needed
to use the sheet. Adam does not track how people use the sheet. This statement
applies to the sheet; do not extend it to Substack or other services.

Paid subscriptions provide optional support for Adam's work. They do not
provide better jobs or earlier feed access. Do not invent paid benefits,
exclusive posts, publishing promises, or personal services.

Use the latest approved support footer supplied by Adam or the current project.
If it is missing, mark `[NEEDS APPROVED SUPPORT FOOTER]` in the draft and state
the gap in the handoff. Do not make up replacement support copy.

## Final review and handoff

Label the output as a draft outside the article. Check the title, subtitle,
names, links, dates, figures, source limits, punctuation, and support copy.
List unresolved checks separately. Do not call a draft ready to publish while
material gaps remain.

Use the voice skill when available. Apply the current weekly-list template
before an older selected-job profile. A checker result does not prove voice,
authorship, or factual accuracy. Do not add opinions to satisfy a profile.

Preparing reports and publishing are separate actions. Use saved inputs for a
writing-only request. Run refresh, catch-up, or publication workflows only when
Adam authorizes them. The [refresh guide](refresh-job-feed-process.md) describes
their effects. Nothing in this guide publishes a post.
